FEED 01Control room, 02:14

The wall in front of me is forty-one screens wide. Loading bay, admin block, the perimeter fence in six segments, a flare stack glowing white in infrared. Somewhere in there is the one frame that matters tonight — a VVIP convoy is fifteen minutes out, and my job is to notice that frame before anything happens in it. This is what a Security Systems Administrator actually does at a plant like Brunei Fertilizer Industries: not watch, exactly. Read forty-one storyless pictures for the one that's about to become a story.

FEED 02What nobody asked

Here's the part that doesn't fit on an org chart. I finished a BSc (Hons) in Computer Science in 2018, after years of diplomas in computing and systems development before it — and then took a job watching camera feeds. Not because the degree didn't work out. Because the first employer to say yes ran a cinema, and I was mid-shift running a film reel and a booking database, both, when I noticed I cared more about why the database did what it did than about the reel.

That noticing didn't go away. It just went quiet for a while: projectionist, then a stretch keeping fire-protection paperwork straight, then an IT apprenticeship at the E-Government National Centre, then CCTV. Systems, every time, whichever kind was on offer.

FEED 03Promotion, 2024

Two years into HSSE at Brunei Fertilizer Industries, I was promoted from CCTV Administrator to CCTV Associate. On paper, a title bump. In practice, the job had quietly become bigger than "CCTV" ever described — cameras are just the system BFI happens to run most of its security through, so managing them means managing most of physical security: access sweeps, VVIP logistics, incident response, the whole apparatus, with a camera feed as the interface.

I'd spent years becoming fluent in a system someone else had built. The old itch came back the moment I noticed nobody had built the software layer this job actually needed.

A person who watches cameras for a living, and quietly writes the software that watches them back.

FEED 04The build

So I built it myself. Not by going back to school, not by waiting for a developer to get assigned to HSSE — by sitting down with the languages I already had and an AI coding partner, and shipping the tool nobody had scoped. I still hold the title Administrator of Security Systems. What I actually do most weeks is closer to a systems architect who also happens to run the physical security desk.

FEED 05Running now

That dashboard has a name now — I call it Guardian — live alongside the quarterly town hall decks I build from scratch each cycle, the access-and-incident reporting nobody else was going to automate, and a home server build queued up next to it in the same spare hours. None of it is on the HSSE org chart. All of it is the actual work.
